# Code of Conduct

ReboundOps is a tool for people going through a job search, which is often a stressful time. We want every space around the project to be welcoming, patient and useful: issues, pull requests, Discussions, the Wiki and any chat about it.

## What we expect

- Be kind and assume good intent. Many people here are between jobs, new to open source, or writing in a second language.
- Keep feedback about the work, not the person. Be specific and suggest a way forward.
- Respect privacy. Do not post anyone's personal information, and do not ask others to share their resume, salary, immigration status or job-search details. Use fictional examples.
- Credit other people's work, and contribute only what you have the right to share.
- Accept a maintainer's decision on scope gracefully. "Not now" is a normal answer in a small project.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, slurs, or demeaning comments about anyone's identity, background, employment status or ability.
- Sexual language or imagery, threats, or encouraging anyone to harm themselves or others.
- Publishing someone's private information (doxxing), or unwelcome contact after being asked to stop.
- Spam, including recruiting, paid "job guarantee" schemes and unrelated promotion.
- Deliberately disrupting discussions, or sustained off-topic arguing.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces, and when someone represents the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

If you experience or see unacceptable behaviour, report it privately:

- Use GitHub's **Report content** option on the comment, issue or discussion. This reaches GitHub and, for repositories, the maintainer.
- Or contact the maintainer privately through the contact details on the maintainer's GitHub profile ([AnkitParekh007](https://github.com/AnkitParekh007)).

Reports are kept confidential. Do not report security vulnerabilities this way; use [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Enforcement

The maintainer reviews every report and may respond with one or more of the following, depending on severity:

1. A private reminder of these expectations.
2. A public or private warning, and removal or editing of the content.
3. A temporary restriction from interacting with the project.
4. A permanent ban for serious or repeated violations.

People who are reported get a chance to explain themselves, unless that would put someone at risk.
