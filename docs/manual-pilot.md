# Manual pilot — facilitator guide

## Goal

Validate whether a recovering software professional can move from uncertainty to **one concrete completed action**. This guide covers a 20–30 minute session and a seven-day follow-up.

## Before the session

- Invite five participants for discovery; aim to facilitate three end-to-end journeys.
- Explain that participation is voluntary, notes are private, and this is an experimental workflow, not employment advice.
- Ask permission before collecting any sensitive personal information. **Do not upload résumés or participant details to a public repository, issue tracker or demo.**
- Use fictional data in public screenshots and demos.
- Provide a convenient way to opt out of quotes or publication.

## Facilitated flow (20–30 minutes)

**0–5 minutes: Constraints.** Ask: What is the hardest decision right now? What work do you want? How urgent is income? How many hours can you realistically devote each week? Capture the answers in the intake form.

**5–10 minutes: Recover evidence.** Ask for three accomplishments, where they can be verified and what remains uncertain. Mark any unsupported claim as unverified; never fill gaps with invented achievements.

**10–17 minutes: Compare choices.** Review Return, Shift and Bridge as hypotheses. Invite the participant to name an actual target role or project, identify supporting evidence, articulate one gap and choose a first test. Use two or three real job descriptions if available, but do not pretend the pilot automatically analyzes them.

**17–25 minutes: Make a plan.** Check estimated time against declared capacity. Revise wording with the participant and choose the smallest first step. The pilot exports an initial plan; manual edits to the Markdown are expected.

**25–30 minutes: Confirm.** Ask: Is the action clear? What could block it? Will the participant keep a copy? Export the Markdown snapshot if they want it.

## Seven-day check-in

Ask which actions were completed, what actually happened and whether anything changed in their view. Record decisions and the reason for each change. Never infer a job offer or attribute hiring success to the tool without evidence.

## Analysis after three journeys

For each experience, evaluate factual accuracy, whether the chosen action was concrete, time budget fit, time to first action, and whether review changed the plan. If the output is impressive but no action happened, revise the workflow before adding more features.
