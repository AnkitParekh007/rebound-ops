# Facilitated pilot kit — no private participant data in GitHub

**Status:** Prepared for owner-run validation. No real participant interviews, outcomes or testimonials are claimed by this document.

## Candidate recruitment and privacy

Recruit five recently laid-off or career-transitioning experienced software professionals for short discovery conversations, then facilitate at least three full ReboundOps journeys. They may be the same people, if appropriate. Invite voluntarily and explain the research purpose and time commitments in advance. No pressure to disclose employment history, income or identifiable personal details.

**Consent introduction (read aloud, adapt to jurisdiction):**

> We're testing whether a structured career recovery plan helps experienced software professionals take one useful next action. Participation is voluntary; you can skip any question or end the session. We'll use fictional examples if you prefer. We won't publish your résumé, interview recording, or identifiable comments without a separate permission request. ReboundOps runs locally, but optional backups contain what you type. AI will remain disabled for this pilot unless we discuss the exact external transfer separately.

Confirm consent before starting. Do not record audio/video without permission. Store any notes privately outside the repository, cloud-synced project folders and public issue threads. Prefer an anonymous participant code.

## Discovery interview (20–30 minutes)

1. Tell me about your first week after deciding to change roles or after losing your job. What was hardest?
2. What information or evidence did you have about your past work? What was missing?
3. Which directions did you consider: returning to a similar role, shifting into an adjacent specialty, or bridge/contract work? What caused indecision?
4. What did you actually do in the last seven days? What prevented additional action?
5. Which tools did you try? What made them useful or distracting?
6. If a tool could improve one decision in this process, what decision would it help you make?
7. What personal information would you refuse to upload to an AI tool?
8. What would count as a useful outcome after one week? Avoid asking participants to endorse a proposed feature before the problem is understood.

Immediately after each interview, record one participant-stated consequential problem, an actual recent action, one contradiction or unanswered question, and a privacy concern. Do not fabricate quotes or automatically interpret silence as agreement.

## Assisted end-to-end journey (30–45 minutes)

1. Explain the three options (similar work, a new kind of work, a stepping-stone role; formerly labelled Return/Shift/Bridge) as things to try, not job-fit predictions.
2. Start with fictional data or user-approved redacted notes.
3. Have the participant correct one claim and identify whether a source can substantiate it.
4. Paste a non-confidential job requirement; mark support/gap/unreviewed explicitly.
5. Choose a route and review time limits, uncertainty and missing proof.
6. Let the participant choose one action they are willing to attempt.
7. Ask them to explain what the export and AI data boundaries mean in their own words.
8. Observe the participant using keyboard/mobile if relevant.
9. Record the chosen action privately, with permission for a seven-day follow-up.

Do not guide the participant toward a favorable route or present a guessed claim as verified. Stop if a participant is distressed or requests privacy.

## Seven-day check-in

Ask what action was actually attempted, whether it was completed, what evidence changed, what obstacles arose, and whether the route remains appropriate. Distinguish *attempted*, *completed*, *useful*, and *employment outcome*; these are not interchangeable.

## Success gate (do not mark complete before real data)

- At least 3 of 5 discovery participants independently describe the same consequential problem.
- Three complete journeys include a participant-chosen next action and a follow-up.
- No invented achievements appear in exports. Uncertain/missing proof remains visible.
- Participants understand confirmed-by-user vs independently verified.
- Time budget is never exceeded.
- Negative and mixed findings are included in the anonymized synthesis, not suppressed.

Participants may optionally fill in **Private pilot feedback** inside their active session and download a Markdown report. This is not telemetry: the answers remain in browser memory and are never uploaded. See the [external pilot runbook](external-pilot-runbook.md) for instructions, recruitment language, follow-up handling and redaction.

Use [the evaluation rubric](evaluation-rubric.md) and the [blank scorecard](pilot-scorecard.md). The owner must review the evidence before approving a public beta.
